## Design Context

### Users
Adults who already eat with intention (often 35–65+) and track meals to audit macros, micronutrients, and longevity topics—not casual calorie logging. They maintain a personal food library and review Mon–Sun days against targets. Primary jobs: log this week’s real meals quickly, scan which days are empty vs filled, and jump into Micro/Longevity analysis.

### Brand Personality
Intentional, clinical-calm, trustworthy. The UI should feel like a precise personal lab notebook: clear structure over decoration; confidence over cheerfulness.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light utilitarian tool UI on warm-neutral canvas (`#f4f4f2`), white surfaces, restrained blue accent (`#3d6b9e`) for “today” / primary actions, teal (`#1f5c53`) for favorites. Preserve existing type, colors, and control patterns—improve hierarchy with space, weight, and framing rather than a new visual brand. Avoid purple gradients, glow, and marketing-card chrome. Density is appropriate for a 7-day diary; breathing room should come from clearer grouping, not sparse empty pages.

### Design Principles
1. **Frame the day** — Each weekday is one readable unit (head + meals + actions); users should know what belongs together at a glance.
2. **Scan left-to-right, day-to-day** — Day identity (weekday + date) outweighs secondary actions; food names outweigh servings chrome.
3. **Group by proximity** — Related controls cluster tightly; distinct regions (week chrome vs day columns vs food rows) separate generously.
4. **Keep every affordance** — Redesigns must not hide Export/Import, Advanced, notes, Copy/Favorite/Clear, Add food / Add Others / Rearrange, or comment/divider semantics.
5. **Clarity over cleverness** — Prefer stronger borders, section headers, and readable type over novel interactions.
